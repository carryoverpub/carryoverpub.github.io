Carryover Effects

Carryover Effects in Reused KV Caches Across Agent Handoffs and Policy Updates

What does a stale KV cache do to the model that reads it?

Workshop on Long-Context Foundation Models, camera-ready 13 November 2026
Paper (13 November) Record: text axis Record: weights axis Cite

Abstract

A carryover effect is what a model shows when it reads a KV cache that another computation wrote: the text or the weights differ from the writer's, and the reader's next-token distribution shifts with them. The systems that serve and train long-horizon agents produce such reads by design, whenever a later stage rebuilds its prompt from text an earlier stage processed, or a pipelined trainer serves rollouts from a cache an earlier policy wrote. We ask what predicts the effect and measure it on two pre-registered records, one for the text axis and one for the weights axis.

On the text axis, the record is 35 coding-agent handoffs whose sender histories run from 35K to 80K tokens. For each, we measure the oracle removal fraction: the share of matched tokens we must remove before the mean key deviation of the remainder falls within a tolerance drawn from a cross-model map's held-out error. Under same-model reuse, the fraction is zero on every one of the 35 handoffs. Its median is zero as well on shorter handoffs, on a second model family, and at two further receivers. Across the four pair cells, the cross-model map must remove 73% to 96% of the matched tokens, and truncating the senders to the short cell's length leaves the deviation above the short cell's level.

On the weights axis, a policy reads a cache that its own earlier weights wrote, up to 32 GRPO steps later. On held-out text, the per-token KL stays inside the registered band on three model families and two seeds. On the policy's own prompts, it sits three to four orders of magnitude higher and is outside the band from the first step, where the stale cache already flips the greedy answer on 71% of GSM8K problems, and on 84% to 90% at later steps. At 32 steps, the stale cache costs the trained policy six of the seven accuracy points it had gained. In the opening windows of the agent trajectories that MLPerf Agentic Inference serves, per-token KL stays within the same band, three orders of magnitude below the policy's own prompts.

At their operating extremes, the two axes land in the same decade: a re-rendered handoff reads at a median of 0.14 nats with 90% greedy agreement, and a policy's own prompts at 32 steps read at 0.09 nats with 90%. The text's relation to the computation that wrote the cache predicts the carryover effect; weight distance, model family, and context length do not.

On the name. In a crossover trial, a carryover effect is the residue of one treatment period on the next. Here the residue is the cache's: a representation of a computation that is not the reader's own, carried into the reader's next token.
